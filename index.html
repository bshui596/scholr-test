Getting started with Scholr 📗

Everything below is also searchable — press ⌘K anytime to jump to a page or run a command.

1 · Set up your semester

Go to Settings → Courses to add every class you're taking with a color for each — that color follows the course everywhere (homework cards, grades, timetable, notes). Then set your default note template in Settings → Profile.

2 · Build your Timetable

In Settings → Timetable, add your class time slots (e.g. "8:35 AM", "Period 1") once. Then go to the Timetable page, click a "+" cell to assign a course to that period on "Day 1", and use ⇉ Copy to Next or + New Day to build out your full rotation (works for both fixed weekly schedules and rotating day-cycle schedules). Mark holidays with 🏖 Off Day.

3 · Use the Calendar

The Calendar is separate from your Timetable — it's for one-off events, deadlines, and appointments, with Month/Week/Day/Agenda views. Click any day to add an event, or open + New Event for repeat rules (daily/weekly/biweekly/monthly/custom) and reminders. Toggle calendars on/off in the left panel to focus on what matters.

4 · Track Homework

The Homework kanban has To Do / In Progress / Done columns. Add a due date and Scholr will flag overdue and due-soon items automatically, and remind you once a day on the Dashboard (turn on 🔔 Enable reminders there for a real notification too).

5 · Take notes in Drive

Notes live in your Drive, organized into folders. The editor supports tables, LaTeX, embedded charts, YouTube, callouts, flashcard blocks, and more from the Insert bar.

6 · Study & track grades

Log marks in Grade Book and see averages update live; use GPA Calculator for what-if planning. Review with Flashcards (spaced by how easy you rate each card) and the Pomodoro timer to structure focus sessions — your streak builds automatically on the Day Plan page.

7 · Share & sync

Your workspace syncs to the cloud automatically (see the ☁️ status pill in the top bar) so it follows you across devices. To share a single note, open it and click 🔗 Share for a 6-digit code anyone can import — treat codes like a link, since anyone who has one can view that note.
